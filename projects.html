Hospitality

Boutique Hotel Lobby

Three stones, one arrival

A lobby is a first impression, so it gets the full range. Cream travertine runs wall-to-wall as a calm floor; a backlit honey-onyx panel turns the reception desk into the room's centre of gravity; and Pietra Grey wraps the columns to anchor the scheme. Bought together and shade-matched as one order, the three stones read as a single, deliberate palette.

Residential

Hillside Villa Facade

Travertine, end to end

A private villa clad entirely in cross-cut cream travertine. Honed panels of consistent tone give the elevation its quiet, monolithic calm, while a brushed finish on the lower courses and steps adds grip and shadow. Travertine's frost tolerance makes it a natural fit for the Armenian climate, and its warmth keeps a large stone facade from ever feeling cold.

Pietra Grey Kitchen

One slab, one statement

The island is the project. A single book of Pietra Grey is cut so the white veining runs unbroken over the top and down the waterfall ends, then polished to a deep gloss against matt cabinetry. Choosing the slab from the bundle in advance is what makes the vein continuous — exactly the kind of detail that gets agreed before anything is cut.

Brick-Clad Block

One blended batch, whole elevation

A mid-rise faced in wire-cut clay brick, blended from a single colour run so the elevation holds together from pavement to parapet. Recessed window reveals use matching special shapes for clean returns, and frost-resistant firing means the face will look the same after a decade of Yerevan winters as it does on handover day.

Onyx Spa Suite

Light through stone

A spa bathroom built around a single backlit green-onyx wall. Resin-backed and bookmatched, the panel glows softly from concealed LEDs while a polished marble floor keeps the rest of the room serene. Onyx used this way is about restraint — one luminous surface does all the work, and everything else stays quiet around it.

Light and Stone: On choosing travertine for contemporary interiors

Travertine is having a moment. Walk through any design fair, scroll any architecture feed — the porous, warm-toned limestone is everywhere, from kitchen splashbacks to gallery floors. But not every travertine is the same material, and not every project deserves the same travertine. After specifying it on a dozen projects across Tehran, Mashhad and Yerevan, here is what we have learned.

What travertine actually is

Travertine forms in hot springs and limestone caves where carbonate minerals precipitate out of solution. The characteristic voids — the "holes" that give the stone its texture — are left by escaping gas bubbles or plant matter that decomposed after being trapped in the mineral. The colour, from bone-white through cream and amber to a deep walnut-brown, depends on the iron content of the source water and the specific quarry.

Iranian travertine — quarried heavily around Mahallat, a city 300 km south of Tehran — is among the finest in the world. It tends to be a warm cream with orange veining, and the voids are fine enough that unfilled versions still feel smooth underhand. We have used it on three projects in the past two years and consider it our default starting point for any floor or wall spec in Iran.

"The material doesn't need to shout. It just needs to be present — to give the light something to work with."

Filled or unfilled

The first decision is always filled versus unfilled. Filled travertine has its voids injected with cement or epoxy and honed flat — it reads almost like a uniform slab of stone, clean and quiet. Unfilled shows the natural surface with all its voids open; it photographs beautifully but collects debris, needs more maintenance, and can be uncomfortable underfoot in large formats.

For floors we almost always specify filled-and-honed, particularly in areas with bare feet: bathrooms, bedrooms, covered terraces. For walls, unfilled is often the better choice — the texture catches raking light and the stone reads as more alive. We used unfilled travertine on the shower walls of the Penthouse Vanak and the effect changes through the day as the sun moves.

Finish

After filled/unfilled, the finish is the second major variable. Honed gives a matte surface that is forgiving, conceals scratches and reads consistently in all lights. Polished produces a mirror-smooth surface that amplifies the depth of the veining but shows every mark; we avoid it in residential projects where the stone will live with real life. Brushed or sandblasted finishes sit between the two — they give the stone a slightly aged, tactile quality that we like particularly for exterior cladding and covered terraces.

Format and direction

Large-format travertine — 800×800 mm or bigger — reads as architectural stone: calm, authoritative, almost structural. Small formats feel more like tile and suit bathrooms or feature walls where the pattern itself becomes the texture. The direction of lay matters too: running the veining vertically on a floor (book-matched or cross-cut) creates a very different rhythm from a natural, horizontal lay.

On the Courtyard House in Mashhad we used 600×1200 mm slabs in a running bond, with the long axis parallel to the main axis of the house, so the floor subtly draws the eye toward the courtyard. It is the kind of decision that is invisible to most visitors but felt by everyone.

Sourcing in Iran and Armenia

For projects in Iran, we work with two quarries in Mahallat and have a long-standing relationship with a fabricator in Tehran who cuts precisely to our drawings. For projects in Yerevan, Armenian basalt and tuff are the first-choice stone for any exterior work — travertine is imported and more expensive, so we use it selectively for interior focal points. The right stone is always the stone that belongs to its place.

Wherever you are sourcing, insist on seeing the actual slab — not a sample tile. Travertine varies within a single batch, and a 10×10 cm chip tells you almost nothing about how a full floor will read. We always specify that slabs are selected in person at the yard, by us or by a trusted local partner.
